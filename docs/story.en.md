## My signature intro 🎤

> My name is Bardiya Kabir — Mr. King of Gods. But you can call me Farland_game.

## My first playground had a power button 🎮

Around age six, my aunt's phone and laptop became my first little learning lab. My twin brother Armiya and I played plenty of games, especially Minecraft, and learned by exploring, downloading and trying things ourselves. Some early experiments taught me lessons the hard way. 😅 Curiosity arrived before the instruction manual.

## Endless: the idea that started it 💡

Games didn't always do what I imagined. I used to wish my father were a game developer so he could build my ideas. Then I asked a better question: why couldn't I learn to make them myself? Around ten or eleven, I imagined a two-player game called **Endless**. That was a childhood game concept, and its story still lives in my memory. The plot can stay in the childhood vault for now. 😄

## A phone became my animation studio 📱🎬

At that point I had limited equipment, English was a barrier and the Persian tutorials I could access were scarce. The game I imagined was beyond the setup I had. I discovered that I could animate on a phone and connected most with **Stick Nodes**. I kept making phone animations through ninth grade. Around fifteen, I got my own laptop and chose computer studies, opening a new stage of my work. Limited tools changed my route; I kept looking for a way to create.

## More tools, bigger projects, real teamwork 🧑‍💻🤝

I expanded into programming and **3D animation with Blender**, then added **After Effects** editing. AI tools now help my learning and workflow too. Friends became collaborators, and the projects grew in scope and length. **Vexland_Manhunt** is a shared creation with **Ali · Alivexor**. My book project, **Visual Animation Tutorial**, also recognises my twin brother **Armiya · ArmiyaFbix**. I have shared what I learned with him, and our creative interests overlap. Today this portfolio brings together software, animation, teaching and ongoing work.

## Kabir, Farland and a name of my own 😎

My family name is **Alizadeh**, but I like being called **Kabir**: a name I chose for myself. **Farland** came from playing with words for a channel name; I learned about Minecraft's Far Lands connection later. I added **game**, leaving room for names such as **Farland_animation**. I began making Aparat videos in seventh grade after getting my first phone, then expanded to YouTube and other platforms with an international audience in mind. The channels are part of a creative identity I want to keep growing.

## Big ambition, a busy quest log 🔥

I like being seen, making an impression and building a name people remember. I'm warm, confident and competitive, and I enjoy organising teams and finding practical, inventive routes through difficult work. Improving how I speak matters to me too; I took speaking classes with Armiya. **Saul Goodman from Better Call Saul** is a personal inspiration for confidence, verbal agility and resourcefulness. Between school, software, animation and group projects, my schedule fills up quickly. Apparently, my “accept new quest” button gets plenty of use. 😄

## My next big scene: Japan 🇯🇵🎬

My next goal is to **study animation at university in Japan**. I hope to earn recognition for my work, collaborate with animation communities and have opportunities to teach as my skills and experience grow. From there, my long-term vision is an **international animation studio with multiple specialist branches**: 3D, 2D, anime and other approaches. I imagine the branches inspiring one another through friendly creative competition, each developing its own strengths. I would like to contribute my own 3D work to Japan's animation community and later build a **game-development studio** too. I want to lead these teams, build successful creative companies and have a meaningful influence on the future of animation and games. Including Japanese in my portfolio reflects that interest and my wish to connect internationally. These are the ambitions I am working toward; this portfolio records the projects along the way.
